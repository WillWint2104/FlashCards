## Concepts (SOURCE)

**Globalisation** is the removal of barriers to trade between nations. It is
characterised by increasing integration between national economies and by a high
degree of transfer across borders of capital (facilities and machinery), labour,
intellectual capital and ideas, financial resources and technology.

The definition is worth taking seriously because of what it lists. Globalisation
is not only goods crossing borders. It is *production factors* crossing borders,
and that is why it reaches the operations function rather than only the sales
figures.

## Explanation (SOURCE)

**Opportunity and threat in the same sentence.** Globalisation gives a business
market opportunities in other nations and gives other nations opportunities in
its market. A competitor applying cost leadership effectively can undercut a
domestic business and dominate. Every advantage globalisation offers is offered
to competitors on the same terms, and an answer that treats it as a menu has
taken half the question.

**What global consumers do to the operations function.** Global consumers seek
global brands and tend to seek standardised products. That preference is what
restructures operations: a business serving them organises around a **global
web**, a network of production sites positioned around the world, with regional
operations, global sourcing and supply chain management holding it together.

Four operations processes are reshaped by targeting a global market:

1. **product design**, which must satisfy global consumers rather than one
   national market
2. **the location of manufacturing facilities**, now a strategic choice rather
   than a domestic given
3. **quality management**, because output from several countries must be
   equivalent
4. **logistics and inventory management**, because the distances are longer and
   the chain has more links

Manufacturing at global scale earns **economies of scale**, and the classic
configuration separates the stages by country: designed in one, manufactured in
another, sold everywhere.

**Services globalise too**, in finance, travel and tourism, software and
telecommunications. The pattern there is standardise the core and vary it
locally: a standardised suite of products with slight variations for local
market needs, so that the back-end processes stay uniform. Consistency of
protocol and internal standard is what makes such a business a viable partner
for other global businesses, which means quality is not a nice-to-have but the
product itself.

**Two configurations, and the contrast is the whole topic.** The source gives
both without ever comparing them:

| the scale configuration | the proximity configuration |
| --- | --- |
| manufacture wherever labour is cheapest | keep the majority of production close to the main market |
| high volume, standardised, long production runs | low volume per line, moved to market quickly |
| economies of scale are the source of advantage | speed and responsiveness are the source of advantage |
| seasonal design cycles | continuous design, new products every few weeks |
| warehousing near markets | central distribution, no local warehousing needed |
| optimises **cost** | optimises **speed** and **flexibility** |

The fast fashion business the source describes takes the second one deliberately
and pays for it: it does not mass produce, and so does not realise economies of
scale that competitors do. Its founder's stated reasons are production proximity
and quick logistics. It still sources fabric globally because that lowers input
cost, and it still keeps stock moving rather than storing it. So the choice is
not global against domestic. It is *which stage* of the process is placed for
cost and which is placed for speed.

That is the single most useful idea in this chapter for essay work, and it is
never stated as a principle.

## Relationships and mechanisms (DERIVED unless marked)

Globalisation is an **influence**, not a strategy: it changes the conditions the
operations manager works in, and the strategies are the response. Keeping that
straight is the difference between answering ops-02 and answering ops-01.

The influence reaches operations through the strategies:

- it makes **global sourcing** possible, which lowers input cost and lengthens
  the chain
- it makes **outsourcing** and offshoring possible, with the control and
  dependability costs those carry
- it forces **quality management** toward international standards, because
  components from several countries must be interchangeable
- it makes **economies of scale** reachable, because one nation's population is
  a ceiling on volume
- it accelerates **scanning and learning**, because practice now crosses borders
  as fast as goods
- it raises the **technology** standard a business must meet, because it is
  competing against whoever is best rather than whoever is nearest

Against performance objectives, globalisation pushes **cost** down and pushes
**dependability** and **flexibility** at risk, because the chain that delivers
the cost saving is longer, crosses jurisdictions, and is exposed to currency
movement. A business that wants speed and flexibility back has to buy them, and
the usual price is giving up some of the scale.

For ops-02, "assess the impact of globalisation on operations management", the
assessment has a clean shape: globalisation lowers cost and raises the standard
of competition simultaneously, so a business that only takes the cost saving
ends up competing on the one dimension where someone will always be cheaper.

## Terminology (SOURCE)

- **globalisation** — the removal of barriers to trade between nations
- **economic integration** — national economies becoming more interdependent
- **global web** — a network of production sites located around the world
- **global consumer** — a customer seeking global brands and standardised
  products
- **standardisation** — producing one specification for all markets
- **localisation / local variation** — varying a standardised product to suit a
  particular market
- **production proximity** — locating production close to the market it serves
- **fast fashion** — an industry model based on short design cycles and rapid
  turnover of stock
- **cost leadership** — competing by being the lowest-cost producer
- **economies of scale** — falling cost per unit as volume rises
- **offshoring** — relocating the business's own operations to another country

## Distinctions and confusions

- **Globalisation is an influence, not a strategy.** It is something happening
  to the business. Global sourcing and global factors are the strategic
  responses. Students answer influence questions with strategies and lose the
  question.
- **Global does not mean standardised everywhere.** The service model given
  standardises the back end and varies the front. Full standardisation is one
  option, not the definition.
- **Cheapest production is not the only global strategy.** Producing close to
  the market and moving stock fast is also a global strategy, and it deliberately
  gives up economies of scale.
- **Economies of scale are not free.** Taking them means long runs of a
  standardised product, which is the least flexible configuration available.
- **Globalisation is symmetrical.** The same open border that gives access to
  cheaper suppliers gives foreign competitors access to the domestic market.
- **Global sourcing is not offshoring.** Sourcing buys inputs abroad; offshoring
  moves the business's own operations there. A business can do either without
  the other.

## Instructional implications (USE)

1. name the aspect of globalisation acting on the business: an open market, a
   supplier pool, a competitor entering, a mobile technology
2. name what it changed in the operations function specifically: design,
   location, quality, logistics, inventory
3. state the mechanism: what became possible or unavoidable that was not before
4. name the performance objective gained and the one exposed
5. judge the impact overall, and include what globalisation did *to* the
   business as well as *for* it

Step 5 is what makes it an assessment rather than a description. Step 2 is what
keeps the answer inside operations, since a student writing about globalisation
in general drifts into economics within two sentences.
