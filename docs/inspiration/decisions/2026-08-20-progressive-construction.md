## What the earlier decision got right

> Do not make students signpost four arguments they have not chosen.

That concern was correct and still is. What it got wrong was the remedy: it
required the four arguments so the signpost would have something to point at.

## The better remedy

Stop requiring the signpost, rather than requiring the four arguments.

```
decode the question → establish a provisional answer → begin wherever is useful
  → plan each argument as you reach it → the plan and the answer emerge together
```

A student needs one thing before writing: **what is one useful thing I could say
about this question.** That is a much smaller cognitive task than designing an
essay, and it is the one they can actually do.

## Three things are now distinct

| | what it is | who owns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the **anchor** | a provisional position or answer, chosen from options or written | the student, changeable |
| the **working answer** | the system's understanding of what the response argues, rebuilt from the arguments in play | the system, derived |
| the **thesis sentence** | prose in the introduction | the student, never touched |

The working answer is assembled from phrases the question authored, one per
pathway, so it is deterministic and costs nothing. A written paragraph counts as
evidence; a planned one counts as intent.

**The system never rewrites the student's thesis.** When the working answer moves
past what they wrote, it says so, shows both, and offers *Revise my thesis* or
*Mine still stands*. That revision is the learning.

## Planning survives as a route, not a stage

Three ways in, all first class: write the introduction, start a body, or plan all
paragraphs first. The full planner is unchanged and one press away. A student who
wants the whole essay laid out can have it; nobody has to.

## Required coverage gates completion, not entry

A question that names its parts shows them up front as information, lets the
student start anywhere, tracks which paragraph addressed which, and names what is
missing **at review**, with the consequence stated and the submit button still
working.

> Planning should make starting easier, never become another task the student has
> to complete before they are allowed to write.

## The shape stays visible

Six compact rows for the six sections, inert. The problem with the old planner was
never that the bodies were visible; it was that their emptiness read as work
demanding completion. Keep the shape, remove the obligation.
