## The decision

```
decode question → understand the core answer → choose the paragraph pathways and
evidence → write the final thesis, informed by that plan → write the essay
```

The student establishes **what the answer is** before planning, and writes the
**polished thesis sentence** after the plan exists.

## Why

Choosing four body pathways before understanding the overall answer is the cart
before the horse. But composing an introduction before knowing what the essay
will contain is the same mistake in the other direction: the signpost has nothing
to point at.

Splitting understanding from composition solves both. Before the plan, the core
relationship is taught with progressive support: *I understand, continue* · *give
me a thesis structure* · *show me an acceptable thesis*. After the plan, the
introduction is written against a response the student has actually decided on.

## What this replaces

P0's flow, which planned the bodies first and then opened the introduction with
the plan in the rail. That stays; a question-understanding and core-answer stage
goes in front of it.
