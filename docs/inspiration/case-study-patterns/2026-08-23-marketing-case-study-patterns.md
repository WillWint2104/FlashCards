Patterns extracted from a textbook case study chapter. **The business in the
pattern source is not the business the app teaches.** Nothing here is evidence,
and no fact below is transferable to McDonald's. See `README.md`.

Each pattern records the shape of the argument, then asks what verified
McDonald's evidence could demonstrate the same relationship, and marks the answer
`mapped`, `partial` or `gap`.

## What the chapter demonstrates about case study writing

Three things worth copying, independent of any particular fact.

**Specificity is at the level of the decision, not the company.** The chapter
never says the business "uses market research". It says which instrument, what
four things the instrument reveals, what was done with the result, and what
followed. That is the level at which a fact becomes usable in a response.

**Every strategy is tied back to a syllabus term in the same breath.** Product
differentiation, positioning, relationship marketing, personal selling, price
points, situational analysis, e-marketing. The case study is written so that a
student reading it collects the vocabulary alongside the fact, which is why a
case study organised by syllabus heading is more useful than one organised
chronologically.

**Weaknesses are stated as plainly as strengths.** The SWOT names outsourced
production, reliance on discounting, narrow markets for some products, exchange
rate vulnerability and the overhead of physical stores. A case study that only
records successes cannot support an evaluate command word, because there is
nothing to weigh.

**What it does badly, worth not copying.** Effects are asserted far more often
than measured. "Sales have grown", "proven effective as demonstrated by growing
sales", "early indications are positive" all appear without figures, so most of
the chapter supports Explain and very little of it supports Evaluate.

## Pattern 1: loyalty scheme as the market research instrument

```
decision   run a membership scheme that requires identification at purchase
process    the membership database yields who buys which range, when, which
           locations, and what else is bought in the same transaction
effect     qualitative follow-up is targeted at the segments the data exposed, a
           range is repositioned for a younger segment, promotion is aligned to it,
           awareness and sales in that category rise
concept    market research (primary data, quantitative then qualitative), the
           marketing process, target markets
HSC use    a complete research-to-strategy-to-result chain, which is the hardest
           thing for a student to find and the most valuable for mkt-02
```

**Why the shape matters.** The database is quantitative and continuous; the
follow-up is qualitative and targeted. The sequence is the teaching point: broad
data identifies where to look, focused research explains what is happening, and
only then does strategy change. A student who can name both stages has described
market research properly rather than saying "they surveyed customers".

**McDonald's mapping: `partial`.** The bank record *App, loyalty rewards and
mobile ordering* establishes that the app carries a loyalty scheme, vouchers,
mobile ordering and a locator, and its `use` text says the app collects customer
data and lets promotions be monitored. That gives the instrument and the data
collection. It does not establish the second half of the chain: a specific
research finding, a specific strategy changed because of it, and a measured
result. The record *Test restaurants and customer feedback surveys* supplies a
different research method (trialling before national launch) and does establish
the compare-before-scaling logic.

**What would close it:** a verified instance of a McDonald's menu or range
decision attributed to app or trial data, with an outcome. Not yet sourced.

## Pattern 2: campaign measured against pre-set brand KPIs

```
decision   run a themed campaign with brand KPIs set beforehand
process    the campaign is executed across channels
effect     reach, views and site visits are reported against the KPIs and the
           campaign is judged to have met them
concept    implementation, monitoring and controlling; KPIs; effectiveness
HSC use    the only pattern in the chapter that supports Evaluate without the
           student supplying the standard themselves
```

**Why the shape matters.** The KPI existed before the result. That is what makes
the judgement an evaluation rather than a description of a large number, and it
is exactly the distinction the reference note on marketing effectiveness turns
on.

**McDonald's mapping: `gap`.** No bank record covers a named McDonald's campaign
with published targets and reported results. *Promotion mix from advertising to
sponsorship* covers the channels used, not the measurement of any one campaign.

**What would close it:** a McDonald's campaign with a published objective and a
published outcome, from corporate or investor material. Recorded as a gap; not
filled by analogy.

## Pattern 3: price points inside a single product line

```
decision   offer the same product category at two or more specifications
process    a basic version for the occasional user and a fuller version for the
           committed user, differing by named features rather than by brand
effect     one product line serves several segments without splitting the brand
concept    pricing strategies, market segmentation, product differentiation
HSC use    the causal step from a target market characteristic to a pricing
           decision, which is what mkt-01 and mkt-04 both ask for
```

**Why the shape matters.** The price difference is justified by a feature
difference that maps onto a segment difference. That three-part link is the
mechanism, and it is what separates this from "they have cheap and expensive
products".

**McDonald's mapping: `mapped`.** *Price points from value range to McCafe*
covers the same relationship: distinct price tiers within one brand serving
different segments. *Segments served by Happy Meal and McCafe* supplies the
segment half.

## Pattern 4: an external shock forcing a channel change, with a second-order
operational benefit

```
decision   respond to a demand shift the business did not choose
process    prior investment in infrastructure allows rapid scaling; a delivery
           partnership is added to solve a fulfilment delay
effect     the immediate problem is solved, and under-used physical sites are
           repurposed as fulfilment points, relieving pressure elsewhere
concept    influences on marketing (economic, government), place and distribution,
           e-marketing, and interdependence with operations
HSC use    an influence-to-strategy-to-effect chain where the effect lands in a
           second function, which is what interdependence questions want
```

**Why the shape matters.** The second-order benefit is the interesting part. The
channel decision was made for marketing reasons and paid off in operations, which
is a genuine interdependence argument rather than a stated one. Note also the
precondition: the business could scale because it had already invested. A student
naming the precondition explains why the same shock damaged competitors.

**McDonald's mapping: `partial`.** *App, loyalty rewards and mobile ordering*
mentions third party delivery platforms, and *Intensive distribution and
standardised service* covers the distribution position. Neither establishes a
change made in response to a specific external event, nor a measured effect, nor
the operational second-order benefit.

**What would close it:** verified McDonald's material on a delivery or ordering
change tied to a stated external cause, with an outcome. Recorded as a gap in the
causal step; the channel facts themselves are already in the bank.

## Pattern 5: segmentation named by attitude as well as demographic

```
decision   divide the market on two axes at once, age and level of commitment
process    six named segments, each with a descriptive label capturing motivation
effect     product range and promotion can be matched to a motivation rather than
           to an age
concept    market segmentation, segmentation variables (demographic and
           psychographic), target markets
HSC use    demonstrates why a segment label must carry a consequence, which is the
           single most useful correction for weak mkt-01 answers
```

**Why the shape matters.** Age alone produces no strategy. Age crossed with
motivation produces a different product, price and message for each cell. This is
the concrete demonstration of the derived claim in the target markets reference
note, that a target market description is only useful if a strategy follows from
it.

**McDonald's mapping: `mapped`.** *Segments served by Happy Meal and McCafe*
establishes distinct segments served by distinct sub-brands within one business.
The psychographic axis is weaker than the pattern source's, so this supports the
concept without matching its sophistication.

## Pattern 6: sustainability as a product attribute and a supply decision at once

```
decision   treat environmental impact as a product characteristic, not a public
           relations activity
process    material choices assessed on manufacturing impact; packaging
           consolidated from many suppliers to one chosen partly for compliance
           reach and sustainability capability; membership of an industry covenant
           that scores participants annually
effect     an external score improves year on year and awards follow; the product
           attribute becomes part of the brand claim
concept    ethical influences on marketing, corporate social responsibility,
           supply chain management, packaging
HSC use    a CSR argument that is operational rather than promotional, which is
           what ops-03 needs and what most student CSR answers lack
```

**Why the shape matters.** Two things are happening at once and students usually
see only one. The environmental claim is a marketing position, and the supplier
consolidation is a supply chain and cost decision. The same act serves both,
which is why it is durable rather than a campaign.

Note also the external scoring body. A CSR claim verified by an outside party is
a stronger evidentiary position than a self-reported target, and that distinction
is worth teaching directly.

**McDonald's mapping: `partial`, and cross-topic.** *Packaging waste, the law and
voluntary action* sits in the operations influences section and covers the
compliance-plus-voluntary-action shape. It is not currently attached to any
marketing pathway, and it does not establish a supplier consolidation or an
externally scored outcome.

**What would close it:** verified McDonald's packaging or sourcing commitments
with third-party verification and a reported result.

## Pattern 7: SWOT that names structural weaknesses

```
decision   publish an internal and external assessment
process    strengths and weaknesses internal, opportunities and threats external
effect     the weaknesses named are structural (outsourced production, reliance on
           discounting, currency exposure, physical store overhead) rather than
           cosmetic
concept    situational analysis, SWOT, influences
HSC use    supplies the counterweight an Evaluate answer needs, and models the
           internal against external distinction students most often get wrong
```

**Why the shape matters.** Three of the four weaknesses are consequences of
strategies listed elsewhere as strengths: outsourcing lowers cost and removes
control, discounting drives volume and trains customers to wait for a sale,
physical stores create experience and carry overhead. A case study is most useful
where its strengths and weaknesses are the same decisions seen twice.

**McDonald's mapping: `gap`.** No bank record carries a McDonald's weakness or a
structural vulnerability. Every marketing record is a capability. This is the
clearest single deficiency in the marketing evidence bank: as it stands, a
student can build Explain answers and cannot build Evaluate answers, because
there is nothing in the bank to weigh against.

**What would close it:** verified material on a McDonald's marketing
vulnerability, for example a documented decline, a withdrawn product, or a
disclosed risk from company reporting. Recorded as a gap.

## Summary of mappings

| pattern | status | what is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 loyalty scheme as research instrument | partial | a finding, a change and a result |
| 2 campaign against pre-set KPIs | gap | any measured McDonald's campaign |
| 3 price points within a line | mapped | nothing |
| 4 shock forcing a channel change | partial | the causal step and the outcome |
| 5 segmentation on two axes | mapped | psychographic axis weaker than the source |
| 6 sustainability as product and supply | partial | supplier decision, external verification |
| 7 SWOT naming structural weaknesses | gap | any weakness at all |

**The finding worth acting on.** Two of the seven map cleanly, three are partial
in the same way, and the two gaps are the same gap: the bank records what
McDonald's *does* and almost never records a *result* or a *cost*. Four of the
seven patterns need a measured outcome that no current record supplies.

That is a bank shaped for Explain. `mkt-02` asks students to assess
effectiveness and `mkt-03` asks to what extent, and neither can be answered from
capability statements alone.

## Next actions this pilot identifies

1. When sourcing the marketing records, prioritise the ones where a result is
   available, since a capability without an outcome cannot support the command
   words two of the four marketing questions use.
2. Add gap records rather than stretching existing ones. Patterns 2 and 7 need
   new records, not rewritten ones.
3. Do not attach patterns 1, 4 and 6 to pathways at full strength. Attach them to
   the part the evidence actually establishes and leave the causal step to the
   Learn layer.
